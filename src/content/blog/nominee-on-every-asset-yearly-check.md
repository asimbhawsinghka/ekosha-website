Naming a nominee takes a few minutes, usually when you open an account or buy a
policy. After that, almost nobody looks at it again.

That is the problem. A nomination made in 2012 does not know that you have since
married, had children, lost a parent or fallen out with a sibling. It stays exactly
as you wrote it, and it is the name your bank, insurer or fund house will go by.

A short check once a year, on every asset that allows a nominee, is one of the most
useful things you can do for your family. Here is how.

## First, what a nominee does (and does not do)

A nominee is the person the institution deals with when the holder has passed away.
In many cases the nominee receives the money or the asset **on behalf of** the legal
heirs. It does not always settle who is finally entitled to it. We cover the
difference in [Nominee vs Legal Heir Explained Simply](/blog/nominee-vs-legal-heir-explained-simply/).

Rules also differ by asset type and change over time, so treat the notes below as a
guide and confirm the current position with the institution. The habit of checking
matters more than any single rule.

## Where to check

The table below lists the common places a nomination can sit. Use it as a list for
your own check.

| Asset | What to look at |
|---|---|
| **Savings and current accounts** | Is a nominee registered? Recent banking law changes allow more than one nominee on deposit accounts, so you can split the order or the shares. |
| **Fixed and recurring deposits** | A nomination is made per deposit, not per bank. A new FD may not carry over the nominee from an older one. |
| **Bank lockers** | The nominee decides who can access the contents. Confirm the nominee and the locker agreement are current. |
| **Life insurance** | Check the nominee and the relationship. Many people never update it after marriage or children. |
| **Health insurance** | Policies without a nominee can leave a claim waiting on paperwork. Add one, and make sure the family knows the policy exists. |
| **Mutual funds** | Nominations are per folio. Fund houses allow up to three nominees with a percentage split. |
| **Stocks and demat** | Nominees are set on the demat account and sometimes on the trading account too. Check both. |
| **PPF, EPF and NPS** | Each has its own nomination, usually online. EPF and NPS are easy to forget because the money sits with an employer or pension account. |
| **Small savings** (NSC, KVP, SCSS, post office schemes) | Nominations are held at the post office or bank branch, and often need a fresh form after changes. |
| **Gold, property and other holdings** | Many do not have a formal nomination, so what matters is a clear record of who should know about them and where the papers are. |

## A simple yearly routine

1. **List everything with a nominee field.** Go through the table above, and include
   accounts held jointly or in a parent's name that you manage.
2. **Write down the current nominee for each.** If you cannot say who it is, that is
   your first finding.
3. **Ask four questions.** Is it still the right person? Are they an adult? Do the
   shares add up to 100%? Does the nominee know?
4. **Update what has changed.** Marriage, a birth, a death, a divorce and a new
   financial year are all good reasons.
5. **Record the date you checked.** Next year's check starts from there.

## The mistakes this check catches

- **A nominee who has passed away,** or whose circumstances have changed.
- **No nominee at all** on an account opened long ago, or on a newer one added in a
  hurry.
- **A minor named as nominee** without anyone nominated to act for them. Many forms
  ask for a guardian.
- **Shares that do not add up** when more than one nominee is named.
- **Different people on related assets,** so a policy and the bank account that pays
  its premium point at different people.
- **A nominee who has never been told.** The name on the form does not help if no one
  knows where to look.
